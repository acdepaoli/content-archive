A CEO said this to me: "I'd pay $900K for a three-year deal."

Not "cut my costs." Not "find me something cheaper." He offered to pay nearly a million dollars for the one thing nobody would sell him. A number that stays put for three years.

Sit with how strange that is. A buyer begging to spend more, and the market has nothing on the shelf.

You already pay for predictability everywhere else. Fixed rates instead of variable. Multi-year contracts. Hedges on fuel, currency, materials. Stable numbers let you plan, price, and borrow.

Then the health plan walks in with a one-year term and a fresh repricing every renewal, and everyone shrugs.

That's not an accident. Every year of volatility creates another negotiation, another commission cycle. For the people who assemble your renewal, volatility is the business model. (You've met the business model. It shows up every October.)

No carrier hands out a guaranteed three-year number. But you can design toward one. Structures that cap your exposure. Contracts that span more than a plan year. Transparency that kills the annual surprise before it gets built. That takes engineering and runway, which is why this work starts mid-year, not at renewal.

That CEO had done the math on what certainty is worth. $900K was his price.

What's yours?
