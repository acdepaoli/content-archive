You know you're a fiduciary on your 401(k).

There's a committee. There are minutes. Somebody benchmarks the funds. The whole apparatus.

Now ask who plays that role for your health plan.

(Go ahead. I'll wait.)

That silence is the whole problem.

Under ERISA and the Consolidated Appropriations Act, health plan sponsors carry real obligations. Select and monitor vendors prudently. Keep fees reasonable. Act in your employees' interest. That duty sits with the sponsor, your company, not your broker. Renewing every October does not discharge it.

Here is the asymmetry: your health plan spends multiples of what your 401(k) match costs, with a fraction of the oversight.

Meeting the duty isn't exotic. You already run this discipline on the retirement side. Know what your advisor is paid, in writing. Benchmark your vendors. Read the contracts before you sign. Document the process.

A calendar and a folder, used consistently. That's most of it.

Who's holding that duty for your health plan right now?
